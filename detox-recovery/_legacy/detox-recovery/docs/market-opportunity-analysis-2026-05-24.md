# Next Step Recovery — Market Opportunity Analysis

**Prepared:** May 2026  
**Business:** Next Step Recovery (nextsteprecovery.com)  
**Stage:** Pre-revenue / just launched  
**Geography:** United States (Year 1–3), potential expansion to English-speaking markets Year 4+  
**Methodology:** Bottom-up primary, top-down validation

---

## Executive Summary

The US substance use disorder (SUD) ecosystem has a structural 80% treatment gap: 52.6 million people needed SUD treatment in 2024, but only 10.2 million received it (SAMHSA 2024 NSDUH). Within that unserved population, the acute withdrawal navigation moment — when someone is in withdrawal, trying to figure out what to do, who to call, and whether they need emergency care — is almost entirely unaddressed by existing services.

Next Step Recovery occupies a specific, underserved niche at the intersection of three growing markets:

1. **Peer support for acute withdrawal navigation** (~$40M TAM): Call-based support specifically for people in or entering withdrawal — not long-term recovery coaching.
2. **Family member education and support** (~$12M TAM): Digital products and calls for family members navigating a loved one's crisis.
3. **B2B patient experience consulting for treatment facilities** (~$25M TAM): Lived-experience advisory to the 17,829 US addiction treatment facilities.

**Combined TAM: ~$120M | SAM: ~$27M | SOM (Year 3): ~$130K–$540K**

The market is growing rapidly: behavioral health telehealth is expanding at 15.83% CAGR; addiction recovery digital tools at 18.7% CAGR. There is no dominant national platform focused on withdrawal-specific peer navigation.

---

## Market Definition

### Problem Being Solved

People in acute withdrawal face a critical navigation failure: they often don't know whether their symptoms are dangerous, what to do next, or how to access appropriate care. Emergency rooms are over-utilized; treatment centers have waitlists; families are uninformed. A non-clinical peer with lived withdrawal experience can triage, navigate, and support — filling the gap between "I'm going through withdrawal" and "I'm in appropriate care."

### Target Customer Profiles

**B2C — Primary: Person in or entering withdrawal**

- Age 18–55, across income levels
- Experiencing withdrawal from opioids, alcohol, stimulants, or polysubstance
- Seeking support but not yet in or unwilling to enter formal treatment
- Using digital channels to find information and support
- Has ability to pay $50–$175 for a call, or download a $9.99 guide

**B2C — Secondary: Family member**

- Adult family member or close friend of someone with SUD
- Seeking guidance on how to help without enabling, escalating, or missing warning signs
- Buyers of digital guides and workshop seats; occasional call bookers

**B2B — Treatment Centers and Recovery Startups**

- Outpatient and residential addiction treatment facilities (>$2M annual revenue)
- Behavioral health startups building recovery-adjacent digital products
- Seeking lived-experience perspective for staff training, patient journey mapping, and communications

### Geographic Scope

United States only for Years 1–3. English-speaking international markets (Canada, UK, Australia) as potential Year 4+ expansion.

## Competitive Landscape

### Direct Competitors (Peer Recovery Coaching)

| Company             | Model                               | Price       | Differentiation vs. Next Step                  |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ----------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **Tempest**         | Expert-led coaching + community     | $41/month   | Sustained sobriety focus, not acute withdrawal |
| **Sober Grid**      | Geolocation peer network + coaching | Per-session | Community model, not navigation specialist     |
| **WEconnect**       | Peer-led meetings + 1:1 coaching    | $40/month   | Subscription model, general recovery           |
| **Marigold Health** | 24/7 peer group support             | Varies      | Groups, not individual navigation calls        |

### Why Next Step Recovery Is Differentiated

1. **Withdrawal-specific focus** — Competitors address long-term sobriety maintenance; Next Step Recovery addresses the acute crisis moment when withdrawal is happening or about to happen.

2. **Navigation role** — The product is "help me figure out what to do next" not "support my ongoing sobriety." This is a fundamentally different job-to-be-done.

3. **Clear non-clinical scope with referral protocol** — 16 defined red-flag conditions trigger immediate escalation. Competitors blur clinical/non-clinical lines; Next Step Recovery draws them explicitly.

4. **B2B patient experience consulting** — No peer recovery coaching platform is selling consulting services to treatment centers. This is an entirely separate market with no direct competitor.

5. **Founder's specific expertise** — Not just "lived experience of addiction" but specific deep knowledge of withdrawal physiology, what it feels like, and what patients and families need in that moment.

### Indirect Competitors (Free Alternatives)

- SAMHSA National Helpline (free, not peer, not call-based)
- Crisis text lines (not withdrawal-specific, not navigational)
- YouTube/TikTok content (free, async, no personalization)
- Primary care physicians (clinical, not available 24/7)
- Al-Anon / SMART Recovery (community, not individual navigation)

The free alternatives are the biggest competition, not the paid platforms. The key value proposition must justify $50+ over free helplines.

## Investment Thesis

### Market Opportunity Assessment

**As a solo/micro practice:** The market is large enough to support a healthy $75K–$150K/year lifestyle business with strong margins. The 80% treatment gap guarantees structural demand for years. This is a compelling micro-business opportunity.

**As a scaled platform:** If the model evolves into a network of trained peer specialists offering withdrawal navigation services nationally, the TAM justifies venture-scale ambition ($10M+ revenue at reasonable market share). No such platform currently exists.

### Key Positives

- **80% structural treatment gap** ensures demand is real and unmet
- **Behavioral health telehealth normalization** removes friction for the product
- **No direct competitor** in withdrawal-specific peer navigation
- **Dual revenue model** (B2C calls + B2B consulting) provides resilience
- **Low capital requirements** — profitable as a solo practice from Month 2–3
- **Strong federal tailwinds** — peer support investment is growing, not shrinking

### Key Risks

- **Free alternatives** — SAMHSA helpline, crisis lines, and online forums serve some of the same need at no cost
- **Scope-of-practice sensitivity** — Misstep in any session could create liability exposure; requires careful boundaries
- **Discovery challenge** — Finding people at the exact moment they're in withdrawal navigating alone is difficult; SEO and referral network are critical
- **Capacity constraint** — Solo founder model caps revenue; scaling requires operational systems

### Venture-Scale Potential

The current model is **not** venture-scale. A solo or small practice does not generate the growth rates or capital efficiency that institutional investors require.

The model **could** become venture-relevant if it becomes:

- A marketplace/platform connecting multiple peer specialists with people in withdrawal
- A B2B SAAS layer for treatment centers (patient navigation tools with lived-experience design)
- A federally-funded peer specialist training and deployment program

None of these require outside investment to test; the solo practice generates the proof points needed to make the case if/when that pivot is desired.

### Recommended Next Steps

1. **Validate B2C unit economics in Months 1–3** — Track actual conversion from fit check → paid call. Target: 30%+ conversion.
2. **Close first B2B engagement** — Use warm relationships to close one project in Months 3–6. This validates the consulting TAM hypothesis.
3. **Build referral network** — Partner with 3–5 treatment intake coordinators to refer patients who are pre-treatment or post-discharge. This is the most capital-efficient customer acquisition channel.
4. **Track SEO organic growth** — Measure monthly organic search traffic to identify whether discovery is happening at the withdrawal moment.
5. **Annual model update** — Revisit this analysis in Q1 2027 with 6–12 months of actual data.
