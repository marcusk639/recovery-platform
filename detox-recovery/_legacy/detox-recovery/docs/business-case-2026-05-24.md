## 1. Problem & Market Opportunity

### The Withdrawal Navigation Gap

Substance use disorder (SUD) affects an estimated **48.7 million Americans** (SAMHSA 2023). Of those, fewer than 10% receive treatment in any given year — not because treatment doesn't exist, but because the path to treatment is fragmented, frightening, and poorly navigated.

**What happens in the gap:**

- A person decides they want to stop using. They don't know what withdrawal will feel like or when it will be dangerous.
- Family members don't know whether to call 911, take them to an ER, or wait it out.
- They Google symptoms and get terrifying results or well-meaning but wrong advice.
- They call a treatment center and get put on a waitlist or given intake paperwork they don't understand.
- They make an appointment but don't show up because shame and fear took over overnight.

This is the **pre-treatment and inter-treatment navigation gap** — and it is where people die, or give up, or spiral. It is entirely non-clinical, but no licensed clinician is paid to operate there.

### Why Lived Experience is the Missing Credential

Current navigation resources fall into two buckets:

1. **Clinical** — crisis lines, ERs, intake coordinators. Appropriate for medical emergencies. Not designed for the 95% of navigation moments that are not emergencies.
2. **Peer recovery coaches** — typically focused on long-term recovery, not acute withdrawal navigation. Often certified under frameworks not designed for the detox/acute-phase period.

**Detox Recovery Support operates in the gap:** A person who has been through withdrawal — multiple substances, multiple treatment attempts — who can say "here's what this feels like, here's what to watch for, here's how to talk to the intake coordinator, here's what your family doesn't know."

This is not clinical care. It is not crisis intervention. It is the navigation support that helps people arrive at clinical care and stay there.

**The dropout problem is expensive:** SUD patients have the highest AMA departure rate of any diagnostic group — 11.71%, versus 1.11% for all inpatients (Manitoba population study, 1990–2009, PMC3853686). Among residential programs, up to 51.4% of patients with opioid co-use prematurely discharge (PMC8692265, 2021). AMA-discharged addiction patients readmit within 14 days at a rate of 25.6%, vs. 3.4% for completers (PMC3169593) — a direct financial burden on treatment centers that this service addresses.

**Veteran sub-market:** Veterans are disproportionately affected by SUD, with elevated rates of poly-substance use driven by the intersection of chronic pain management, PTSD, and military culture around alcohol. The VA system serves ~9M enrolled veterans; an estimated 10–11% screen positive for SUD. Critically, veterans often experience the same multi-substance withdrawal profile (opioid + benzo + alcohol) that this navigator has personal experience with — making this one of the highest-fit segments for lived-experience peer support. The VA Community Care program creates a formal referral infrastructure through which VA providers can route veterans to community-based peer support services.

Conservatively 1–2M people annually in the decision-to-enter-treatment window. Veterans represent a high-priority sub-segment with institutional referral infrastructure (VA, VSOs) not available for the general population.

## 3. Competitive Landscape

### The Competitive Gap

Research across the existing landscape finds **no identified company specifically positioned as a non-clinical, lived-experience peer navigation service for the active withdrawal window** before or between formal treatment episodes. This gap is confirmed by a 2023 proof-of-concept pilot (PMC10357372) testing a "digitally-assisted peer recovery coach" for post-withdrawal linkage to outpatient care — the researchers explicitly noted the gap; no scaled commercial product emerged from their work.

### Direct Alternatives and Their Gaps

| Category                      | Examples                              | Funding       | Gap They Leave                                                      |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Crisis lines                  | SAMHSA helpline, 988 Lifeline         | Gov. funded   | Designed for emergencies; not "I'm deciding whether to go to detox" |
| Peer recovery coaches         | CCAR-certified coaches                | Variable      | Focused on sustained recovery; rarely cover acute withdrawal phase  |
| Treatment intake coordinators | In-house at treatment centers         | —             | Conflict of interest — they represent one program                   |
| Online forums                 | r/quittingkratom, r/Suboxone          | —             | Unmoderated, no accountability, no individual guidance              |
| Workit Health                 | Virtual MAT + therapy, 9 states       | $135M raised  | Clinical (buprenorphine prescribing); not pre-treatment navigation  |
| Eleanor Health                | Hybrid SUD + mental health, 15 states | Undisclosed   | Clinical model; not non-clinical peer navigation                    |
| Pelago (fmr. Quit Genius)     | Virtual clinic, employer-contracted   | $58M raised   | Clinical/CBT; not peer-led; not withdrawal-phase                    |
| Bicycle Health                | Virtual OUD-focused MAT               | $16.5M (2025) | Opioid-only; clinical model                                         |
| Sober Sidekick                | Peer connection app                   | $7.6M raised  | Relapse prevention and ongoing sobriety; not withdrawal navigation  |
| Tempest                       | Alcohol recovery coaching             | $14M raised   | Post-detox; not withdrawal-phase                                    |

**Pattern across all digital health incumbents:** Every major player operates as a clinical or quasi-clinical service requiring licensure, prescriber infrastructure, or insurance billing. None is positioned in the non-clinical, peer-navigated, withdrawal-phase window. Treatment centers are the customers for clinical platforms. People in active withdrawal _before_ making the call to enter treatment are not the primary customer for any of these services.

### Key Differentiators

1. **Lived experience** — not a certification, not a credential. The navigation authority comes from having been through opioid, benzodiazepine, alcohol, and stimulant withdrawal personally.
2. **Hard clinical boundary** — the referral logic is explicit, public, and enforced in the product code. This earns trust from clinical partners who worry about scope creep.
3. **Pre-treatment and between-treatment focus** — specifically designed for the 72-hour to 7-day window nobody currently owns.
4. **Low-friction, low-cost access** — $50 for 30 minutes is accessible and subsidizable via donations.
5. **Non-commercial navigation** — the fit check routes people _away_ from this service when they need emergency or medical care. No retention incentive for clinical cases.

## 4. Go-to-Market: Partnership Strategy

### Partnership Model A: Treatment Center Referral Network

**Who:** Residential detox centers, outpatient programs, MAT/MOUD clinics, sober living operators

**Value exchange:**

- Treatment center refers clients in the pre-admission window (inquiry → intake)
- We help the client arrive prepared, less frightened, and more likely to complete intake
- Treatment center sees improved show rates and reduced early AMA departures

**Revenue model options:**

- Flat referral fee per converted intake (paid to treatment center)
- Reciprocal referral (we route clinical-needs clients to partner programs)
- Revenue share on consulting engagements sourced through the relationship

**Integration touchpoints:**

- Co-branded "what to expect at detox" materials using our patient education review framework
- Warm handoff protocol: client finishes a navigation call with us, receives warm intro to intake coordinator

---

### Partnership Model B: Staff Training & Quality Improvement

**Who:** Treatment programs with high dropout, AMA, or patient complaint rates; behavioral health organizations; hospital ED social work departments

**Value exchange:**

- We deliver patient-experience training, journey mapping, and communication workshops
- Program gains perspective they cannot get from clinical staff alone
- Measurable outcomes: AMA rate, patient satisfaction scores, 30-day readmission

**Revenue model:**

- Project-based consulting ($2,000–$8,000 per engagement)
- Ongoing retainer for annual staff training cycles
- Speaking at program retreats, grand rounds, clinical conferences

**Why this makes sense now:** AMA departure rates from detox units range from 17–40% depending on substance and program. A 5–10 percentage point reduction for a mid-size residential program represents material financial impact — making consulting ROI demonstrable.

---

### Partnership Model C: Digital Health Startup Advisory

**Who:** Seed-to-Series B startups building in recovery, MOUD, peer support, behavioral health, or detox navigation

**Value exchange:**

- Access to lived-experience advisory throughout product design and launch
- Patient-voice validation for investor pitches and clinical partnerships
- Insight into: language patients reject, UI patterns that increase shame, onboarding flows that feel coercive

**Revenue model:**

- Monthly advisory retainer
- Equity advisory (small %, for appropriate-stage companies)
- Project-based UX/copy review

---

### Partnership Model D: VA and Military Veteran Networks

**Who:** VA Medical Centers (VAMCs), Vet Centers, Veterans Service Organizations (VFW, American Legion, DAV), state Departments of Veterans Affairs, military family support programs

**The access advantage:** As a US military veteran, the founder has institutional access to partnership channels that are not available to non-veteran operators. VAMCs route veterans to community-based peer support through the VA Community Care program — a formal referral infrastructure with billing pathways. VSO chapters (VFW, American Legion) represent community infrastructure reaching veterans who have not yet engaged with VA care or formal treatment, exactly the pre-treatment navigation gap this service fills.

**Value exchange:**

- VAMCs and Vet Centers can refer veterans needing non-clinical withdrawal navigation to a veteran peer specialist who understands the specific poly-substance profile common in this population
- VSO posts can host educational workshops and distribute resources to members who are not yet treatment-seeking
- Military family programs can use the service for family navigation support — families of veterans often face the same "what do I do" crisis as civilian families

**Unique fit:** Veterans disproportionately present with opioid + benzodiazepine + alcohol poly-substance use — the exact multi-substance withdrawal profile the founder has navigated personally. This specificity of lived experience is rare and recognized by VA behavioral health staff as meaningful.

**Credential pathway:** VA Peer Support Specialist certification (open to veterans in recovery enrolled in VA healthcare) is the formal credential that makes VA partnership and reimbursement pathways accessible. Pursuing this certification is a near-term milestone with direct business value.

**Funding pathway:** SAMHSA's Veterans and Military Families initiative, VA peer support program grants, Bob Woodruff Foundation, Gary Sinise Foundation, and state veteran affairs departments all fund peer support work for veterans — representing non-dilutive grant capital not available to non-veteran competitors.

**Revenue model:**

- VA Community Care billing (once VA Peer Support Specialist certified)
- Grant-funded veteran outreach and education programs
- VSO-sponsored workshops and materials distribution
- Speaking at veteran health conferences and stand-down events

## Appendix: Clinical Boundary Reference

The following conditions trigger immediate referral to emergency or medical care. This platform does not provide support calls to anyone experiencing these symptoms:

- Alcohol withdrawal
- Benzodiazepine withdrawal
- Barbiturate withdrawal
- GHB or GBL withdrawal
- Phenibut withdrawal
- History of seizures
- Hallucinations
- Delirium or acute confusion
- Pregnancy
- Suicidal ideation
- Chest pain
- Fainting or loss of consciousness
- Severe dehydration
- Unstable vital signs
- Severe psychiatric symptoms
- Complex polysubstance withdrawal

_This boundary is enforced in the product's `REFERRAL_CONDITIONS` constant and is surfaced to users before any service interaction begins._
