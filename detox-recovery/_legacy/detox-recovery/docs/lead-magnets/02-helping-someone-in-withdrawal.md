## Guide (PDF body)

### Why this guide exists

You love someone who is in withdrawal, or about to be. You are not a clinician. You are not their sponsor. You are someone who is trying to help while also being scared, tired, and probably angry — and the internet is not great at telling you what to actually do.

This guide is written from the patient side of the experience. [personalize — 1–2 sentences about what your family did or didn't do that mattered]. It will not tell you how to make them quit. It will tell you how to be useful without making things worse.

This is not medical advice. It does not assess whether their withdrawal is safe to manage outside a medical setting. It will, however, tell you when to stop being a supporter and start being someone who calls for help.

---

### Section 1 — Call 911 / get to an ER if any of these are true

These override everything else in this guide. If the person you love is showing any of these signs, **stop reading and act:**

- **Seizure** — actively having one, or just had one
- **Withdrawing from alcohol, benzodiazepines (Xanax, Valium, Klonopin, Ativan), barbiturates, GHB/GBL, or phenibut** — these can cause seizures and need medical supervision
- **History of seizures** in their withdrawal history
- **Hallucinations** — seeing, hearing, or feeling things that aren't there
- **Delirium or acute confusion** — can't track time, place, or who you are
- **Suicidal statements or behavior** — including indirect ones ("everyone would be better off…")
- **Chest pain or pressure**
- **Fainted or lost consciousness**
- **Severe dehydration** — can't keep water down for hours, hasn't urinated in 12+ hours
- **Vital signs that look wrong** — racing pulse, very high or low blood pressure, blue lips, gray skin
- **Severe psychiatric symptoms** — panic that won't stop, paranoia, mania
- **Pregnancy** plus active withdrawal
- **Multiple substances** in active use or withdrawal at once

You don't need their permission. You don't need to be sure. If you would call 911 for any other adult showing these signs, do it now.

**US numbers:**

- Emergency: **911**
- Suicide & Crisis Lifeline: **988**
- SAMHSA National Helpline: **1-800-662-4357**

### Section 2 — Things that almost always make withdrawal worse

These are the patterns that good people fall into without realizing. None of them are about love. They're about fear.

**Shame, dressed up as concern.**
"How could you do this to us." "After everything we've done." "You promised."
What the other person hears: _I am a problem to be solved. I have failed you again._
Result: they shut down, lie, or use to escape the conversation.

**Surveillance.**
Going through their phone. Counting pills. Watching them swallow. Checking their breath.
What the other person hears: _I am not a person, I am a suspect._
Result: they get better at hiding, not better at recovering.

**Ultimatums you can't enforce.**
"If you use one more time, I'm leaving." (and you don't leave)
"If you don't go to treatment, I'm cutting you off." (and you don't)
What the other person hears: _Words are cheap here. Nothing has to change._
Result: every future ultimatum loses weight.

**"Just say no" framing.**
Treating it as a willpower problem. Suggesting they should "just stop." Comparing them to someone who quit.
What the other person hears: _You don't believe this is real._
Result: they stop talking to you about it.

**Bailing them out of every consequence.**
Paying their rent so they don't get evicted while still using. Calling their boss to cover for them. Driving them to get a refill of something they're abusing.
What the other person hears: _I don't have to change because nothing actually breaks._
Result: the cost of using stays artificially low.

None of this is about being harsh. It's about not feeding a pattern that's killing them.

### Section 3 — Things that actually help

**Show up without an agenda.**
Sometimes the right move is bringing food and not bringing up the substance. The relationship is not a vehicle for fixing them; it's the thing that survives the fixing.

**Be specific.**
Not: "Let me know if you need anything."
Yes: "I'm coming Tuesday at 6 with dinner. You don't have to talk."

**Make the next concrete step easier than not taking it.**
Not: "You should call a doctor."
Yes: "I have the number open on my phone. Want me to dial and hand you the phone?"

**Drive them places.**
The cost of getting to a treatment intake, a doctor, an AA meeting, a methadone clinic — at hour 30 of withdrawal — is enormous. If you can drive, drive.

**Manage the boring stuff.**
Hydration. Food they can actually keep down (broth, crackers, electrolytes). A clean enough space. Charged phone. Quiet.

**Be the keeper of the plan.**
When they're inside the experience, they can't hold the plan in their head. You can. "We said if X happens, we go to the ER. X is happening. Let's go."

**Tell the truth about what you're feeling.**
"I'm scared. I'm not leaving. I don't know what to do either, and I'm going to find out."

**Take care of yourself in public.**
If you fall apart, your support stops. Sleep. Eat. Call your own people. Therapy is for you too.

### Section 4 — What to say when you don't know what to say

Steal these. Modify them. They work because they are direct, low-shame, and don't pretend to know things you don't.

| Instead of                                 | Try                                                                |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| "Why would you do this?"                   | "I want to understand what's happening for you."                   |
| "You need to go to rehab."                 | "I'm going to help you figure out what kind of care fits."         |
| "You promised you'd stop."                 | "I know you wanted to stop. I want to help you with what's next."  |
| "I can't believe you're doing this again." | "Whatever happens next, I'm here. What do you actually need now?"  |
| "How can you do this to your kids?"        | "Your kids love you. So do I. Let's figure out the next step."     |
| "You're going to die if you don't stop."   | "I'm scared I'm going to lose you. I need us to make a plan."      |
| "Have you tried just not drinking?"        | _(say nothing; sit with them)_                                     |
| "I read this article…"                     | "I read something — can I share it later when you have bandwidth?" |

When in doubt: fewer words. Presence over commentary.

### Section 5 — How to know which level of care to push for

You are not deciding for them. You are helping them see what fits.

| If the person is…                                                    | The right next step is usually…           |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Showing any condition in Section 1                                   | ER, today                                 |
| Withdrawing from alcohol/benzos/barbiturates and not yet symptomatic | Medical detox program                     |
| Past acute withdrawal and stable                                     | Outpatient treatment, IOP, or therapist   |
| Asking for help and you're not sure what kind                        | A free fit check is built for this moment |
| Refusing all help                                                    | Stay connected, hold your boundary, wait  |
| Refusing all help AND in active danger                               | ER — they can be evaluated involuntarily  |
| Stable but lonely and undirected                                     | Peer support, 12-step, SMART, community   |

### Section 6 — Taking care of yourself

This is not a side note. If you fall apart, your support stops.

- **Get your own support.** Al-Anon, Nar-Anon, SMART Friends & Family, therapy, a friend who's been through this. You need a place to be angry and tired without it landing on the person in withdrawal.
- **Sleep.** Even four hours in a row is better than the alternative. Trade off with another family member if you can.
- **Eat regular food, not what's around.** Withdrawal kitchens fill up with junk because nobody cooks. You need protein and water too.
- **Hold one thing back for yourself.** A walk. A coffee. A 20-minute show. Something that is yours and not contingent on how their day is going.
- **Decide what you're willing to do — and not.** Write it down. Read it when you forget. "I will drive to appointments. I will not lie to their boss." This is not coldness. This is sustainability.
