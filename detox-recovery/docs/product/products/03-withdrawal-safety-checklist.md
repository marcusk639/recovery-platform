## Section 1 — Emergency triggers (stop and go to ER)

**If you check any single box below, stop. Go to an emergency room or call 911.**
**Do not finish the rest of this checklist first.**

```
☐ I am withdrawing from alcohol
☐ I am withdrawing from a benzodiazepine
   (Xanax / Valium / Klonopin / Ativan / others)
☐ I am withdrawing from a barbiturate
☐ I am withdrawing from GHB or GBL
☐ I am withdrawing from phenibut
☐ I have a history of seizures
☐ I am seeing, hearing, or feeling things that aren't there
☐ I feel confused or can't track where I am or what time it is
☐ I am pregnant
☐ I am having thoughts of suicide or self-harm
☐ I have chest pain or pressure
☐ I have fainted or lost consciousness recently
☐ I cannot keep water down and feel severely dehydrated
☐ My heart is racing or pounding in a way that scares me
☐ I have severe panic, paranoia, or other psychiatric symptoms
☐ I am withdrawing from multiple substances at once
```

**If any box above is checked, stop now and get to emergency care.**

## Section 6 — Decision aid

A simple framework, **not** a safety determination.

```
1. Have you checked any box in Section 1?
   ☐ Yes  →  GO TO ER NOW. DO NOT WAIT.
   ☐ No   →  Continue.

2. Are you alone, or is someone with you?
   ☐ Alone, and no one knows I'm withdrawing →
      Tell one person now. Don't continue until you have.
   ☐ Someone knows / is with me. Continue.

3. Looking at Sections 3 and 4 — are your symptoms
   ☐ Getting better hour by hour
   ☐ Roughly the same
   ☐ Getting worse  →
      That is a signal. Talk to a clinician today (urgent care,
      addiction medicine, tele-MAT, or a fit check).

4. Can you keep water down?
   ☐ Yes
   ☐ No  →  See a clinician today.

5. Have you slept at all in the last 48 hours?
   ☐ Yes
   ☐ No  →  See a clinician today.

6. Are you having any new symptoms today that weren't there yesterday?
   ☐ No
   ☐ Yes  →  Note what they are. Tell a clinician.

7. Have you had withdrawal before, and is this one worse?
   ☐ This is my first
   ☐ I've had withdrawal before; this one feels similar
   ☐ I've had withdrawal before; this one feels worse  →
      That is a signal. Treat it as an escalation.

8. Do you have a plan if you get worse in the next 6 hours?
   ☐ Yes — what is it: __________________________________
   ☐ No  →  Make one before continuing. (Phone number,
            person, address of nearest ER.)
```

## Closing

This checklist did not make you safer. _Going to a clinician with it did._

The hardest part of withdrawal is that the part of your brain that decides "is this serious enough to seek help" is the part that's most impaired by the withdrawal itself. This checklist is a way to outsource some of that decision-making to a calmer version of yourself — the one who is filling this out, not the one who's deciding whether to call in three hours.
