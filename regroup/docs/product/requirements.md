The most critical features are the ones that (1) reduce operator admin load and (2) make resident behavior visible and structured in day‑to‑day life.[1][2]

## Resident & house management

- **Resident profiles**: Intake info, emergency contacts, admit/discharge dates, legal status, meds, privileges, and documents, all in one place.[2][3][1]
- **Property/bed management**: Houses, rooms, beds, occupancy status, waitlist, and simple admissions workflow so you always know who is where and what’s available.[3][4][5]
- **Rules, phases, and privileges**: Configurable house rules, levels/phases, curfew, passes/overnights, and privileges tied to compliance and behavior.[2][3]

These give operators a clear operational backbone and reduce spreadsheet/whiteboard chaos.[4][5]

## Accountability tooling

- **Chores and task scheduling**: Assign chores, track completion, and surface missed chores; ideally with resident self‑logging plus staff verification.[6][2]
- **Meeting and curfew tracking**: Log required external meetings (AA/NA/etc.), attendance, and house check‑ins/curfew compliance.[7][3][6]
- **UA/BA and incident logging**: Track drug/alcohol test results, infractions, crises, and notes in a structured way tied to each resident.[4][2]

Accountability in sober living communities is built from structured routines, clear rules, peer visibility, and consistent follow‑through; your app’s core should mirror that.[8][7]

## Money and admin

- **Rent and payment tracking**: Invoices, balances, online payments, and receipts, with alerts for late or missed payments.[5][1][2]
- **Reporting & outcomes**: Basic dashboards for occupancy, length of stay, infractions, discharges, and key outcomes, to support grants, referrals, and oversight.[1][3][2]
- **Document & form management**: E‑sign intake packets, house agreements, and policy docs; store everything in the resident chart.[9][3][1]

These features directly support financial stability and compliance, which are big buying drivers for operators.[3][5]

## Communication

- **Staff notes & shift logs**: Shared notes per resident and per house (shift reports, incident follow‑up, case updates).[3][4]
- **Announcements & messaging (lightweight)**: House‑wide announcements and reminders for chores, meetings, and curfew expectations.

Nice‑to‑have resident‑facing extras (mood logs, goals, journaling, self‑logged events) can come later; focus first on the operational spine and the few accountability behaviors operators are already trying to track by hand.[9][6][8]
